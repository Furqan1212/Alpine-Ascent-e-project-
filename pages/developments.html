The busiest Everest ever.

Spring 2026

A record season, and a warning.

Nepal reported more than 1,000 summits of Everest in spring 2026, the most in a single season. The numbers brought celebration, and fresh calls for limits.

How it unfolded

The season, in order.

April

A late start

A huge serac hanging over the route delayed the season until Nepali climbers fixed an alternative line.

12 May

Ski descents without support

Polish skier Bartek Ziemski reportedly climbed Lhotse and then Everest without Sherpa support or bottled oxygen, skiing down both.

20–21 May

A single-day record

About 274 climbers summited from the Nepal side in one weather window, beating the 2019 record of 223.

Late May

Records for the veterans

Kami Rita Sherpa reached his 32nd summit, and Lhakpa Sherpa her 11th, a record for a woman.

June

The cost and the debate

At least five people died and a Nepali guide went missing. Guides called for limits, and proposed rules would demand prior 7,000 m experience for permits.

What it means

Three trends to watch.

Crowding

Permits at record highs

Nepal issued close to 500 permits for foreign climbers even after the royalty rose to $15,000.

Safety

Experience rules

Nepal has proposed that climbers prove themselves on a 7,000 m peak before attempting Everest.

Stewardship

Care for the people who carry

Sherpa guides did the heavy lifting of this season and are asking for better protection and rescue standards.
