Climb well. Come home.

Golden rules

Three habits of safe climbers.

Rule 01

Turn back on time

The summit is optional. Reaching the bottom is mandatory.

Rule 02

Acclimatise slowly

Climb high, sleep low, and rest. Descend at the first sign of serious altitude illness.

Rule 03

Never climb alone untrained

Partners, guides and clear plans catch the mistakes you cannot see.

Leave no trace

Seven principles for fragile places.

The mountains are shared by everyone, and by everyone who comes after you.

1

Plan ahead and prepare

Know the rules, weather and terrain. Good planning prevents most emergencies.

2

Use durable surfaces

Camp and walk on rock, snow or established paths to protect fragile ground.

3

Dispose of waste properly

Pack out all rubbish. Follow local rules for human waste, which is often carried out at high camps.

4

Leave what you find

Leave rocks, plants and historic objects where they are.

5

Minimise campfire impact

Use a stove instead of a fire. Wood is scarce high in the mountains.

6

Respect wildlife

Observe from a distance and never feed animals.

7

Be considerate of others

Share trails and huts, keep noise down and be kind to local communities.

Questions

Worth knowing.

What should I pack first?

Start with layers, boots that fit, a headlamp, water and a first-aid kit. Add technical gear for your route.

How early should I start training?

Three to six months before a big trip is a good rule, and longer for expedition peaks.

What if the weather turns?

Turn back early, keep your team together, and shelter if you cannot descend safely.
