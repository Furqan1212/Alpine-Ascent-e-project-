Three centuries of summits.

Milestones

How we got here.

1786

Mont Blanc first climbed

Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard reach the top of Western Europe’s highest peak, marking the birth of modern alpinism.

1857

The Alpine Club is founded

Climbers in London form the world’s first mountaineering club, setting the pattern for clubs everywhere.

1865

The Matterhorn conquered

Edward Whymper’s party summits on 14 July, but four climbers die on the descent, a defining moment for climbing safety.

1950

Annapurna: the first 8,000er

Maurice Herzog and Louis Lachenal reach the summit of Annapurna I, the first of the fourteen to be climbed.

1953

Everest summited

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay stand on the roof of the world, ending a decades-long race.

1978

Everest without oxygen

Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler prove the summit possible without supplemental oxygen.

1980

Everest solo

Messner climbs Everest alone and without bottled oxygen, an achievement that reshaped what climbers thought possible.

2021

K2 in winter

A Nepali team makes the first winter ascent of K2, the last 8,000-metre peak to fall in the cold season.

2026

A record Everest season

Nepal reports more than 1,000 summits in a single spring season, along with new debate over crowding.

Questions

Worth knowing.

Who climbed Everest first?

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reached the summit on 29 May 1953 as part of a British expedition.

Why was Mont Blanc important?

Its first ascent in 1786 is often treated as the start of modern mountaineering.

What was the golden age of Himalayan climbing?

The years from 1950 to 1964, when all fourteen 8,000-metre peaks were first climbed.
