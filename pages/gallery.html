Photography tips

Shoot the mountains well.

Four habits that turn holiday snaps into photographs worth framing.

1

Chase the light

Shoot in the golden hour after sunrise and before sunset. Low sun adds depth and warm colour to snow and rock.

Check sunrise times and be in position early.

2

Compose with care

Place a person in the frame for scale, use ridgelines as leading lines, and keep a foreground element for depth.

The rule of thirds works well for big landscapes.

3

Look after your kit

Cold drains batteries fast. Keep spares inside your jacket, and seal your camera in a bag before moving indoors to avoid condensation.

Bright snow fools meters, so add about one stop of exposure.

4

Stay safe first

Never step near cliff edges or snow cornices for a picture. Stop moving before you shoot and keep your team in view.

No photograph is worth a fall.

Questions

Worth knowing.

Can I use the photos on this site?

The photographs come from Unsplash. Check each photographer’s licence before reusing them elsewhere.

Can I watch the expedition films?

Yes. Choose the Video filter above, then select a film to play it full-screen. The films stream from YouTube, so you need an internet connection.

Which format is best for sharing?

JPEG is fine for sharing. Shoot RAW if you plan to edit your photos later.
