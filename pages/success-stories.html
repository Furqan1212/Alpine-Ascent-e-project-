Summits that changed lives.

Expedition success stories

Proof that it can be done.

Four expeditions, four lessons. Each began with a team, a plan and the courage to keep going.

1953

Everest, at last.

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reach the summit at 11:30 a.m. on 29 May.

The British expedition, led by John Hunt, spent weeks building camps and ferrying loads before the final push. Hillary and Tenzing climbed the last section together and spent only a quarter of an hour on top.

  • PeakMount Everest, 8,849 m
  • RouteSouth Col, Nepal
  • LessonTeamwork and logistics win summits
1975

First woman on Everest.

Junko Tabei of Japan reaches the summit on 16 May, ten days after an avalanche buried her camp.

She was dug out of the snow and climbed on. Tabei later became the first woman to complete the Seven Summits, and she founded a ladies climbing club in Tokyo.

  • PeakMount Everest
  • TeamJapanese Ladies Expedition
  • LessonResilience after setbacks
2001

Climbing without sight.

Erik Weihenmayer becomes the first blind person to summit Everest.

Weihenmayer followed bells on his teammates’ packs and the clink of their ice axes. He later completed the Seven Summits, proving that preparation and trust matter more than sight.

  • PeakMount Everest
  • MethodBell guides and voice calls
  • LessonSystems make the impossible repeatable
2016

Pakistan’s winter first.

Muhammad Ali Sadpara, Simone Moro and Alex Txikon summit Nanga Parbat in winter.

Nanga Parbat had defeated winter attempts for decades. The trio reached the top on 26 February after weeks of storms, becoming the first to do it in the coldest season.

  • PeakNanga Parbat, 8,126 m
  • CountryPakistan
  • LessonPatience and a weather window

Questions

Worth knowing.

Where can I read more about these climbs?

Search for the climbers by name. Their books, films and interviews give much richer detail than any summary.

Why are these stories included?

They show how preparation, teamwork and patience turn a hard goal into a safe success.

Can ordinary people climb Everest?

Some do with guides and years of preparation, but it remains dangerous and expensive. Smaller peaks are a better first step.
