Fundamentals

Movement on rope and ice.

Good technique turns a dangerous objective into a manageable one. Every climber starts with the same core skills.

Roped teams protect each other on glaciers and steep ground, while crampons and ice axes provide purchase on snow and ice. Practise each skill on safe terrain until it is automatic.

  • BelayingControlling the rope to arrest a fall
  • Self-arrestStopping a slide with the ice axe pick
  • Front-pointingKicking crampon points into steep ice
  • RappellingDescending a rope in a controlled way

Step by step

Self-arrest in four moves.

If you slide on a steep snow slope, these four moves can stop you. Learn them with an instructor on a safe slope.

1

Grip the axe

Hold the axe with one hand over the head, thumb under the adze, and the other hand near the spike on the shaft. Keep it across your chest as you climb.

Practise carrying it this way until it is automatic.

2

Roll onto the axe

As soon as you slide, roll toward the pick side so your chest lands on the axe head. Speed matters, because a slide gains pace quickly.

Shout to your team, so they can brace on the rope.

3

Dig the pick in

Pull the axe hard against your chest and shoulder so the pick bites the snow. Keep your weight over the head of the axe and your body stretched out.

Keep your hips low and your feet below you.

4

Keep your feet up

When wearing crampons, lift your feet clear of the snow so the points do not catch and flip you. Once you stop, kick in and dig a stance.

Do not stand up until you are fully stopped and secure.

Knots to know

Four knots that matter.

Learn these on the ground until you can tie them in the dark with cold hands.

  • Figure-eight follow-throughTies the rope to your harness. Strong, easy to check and the standard for climbers.
  • Clove hitchQuickly clips the rope to an anchor and is easy to adjust.
  • PrusikA friction knot that grips the rope under load and slides when released, used for rescue.
  • Munter hitchA friction hitch for belaying and abseiling when a device is not available.

Questions

Worth knowing.

How long does it take to learn rope work?

Basic skills take a few days on a course, but confidence takes seasons. Learn with a qualified instructor.

Do I need crampons and an ice axe for every mountain?

Only on snow and ice. Many classic walking summits need neither, but check conditions before you go.

What is the most important habit?

Partner checks: look at each other’s knots, harnesses and anchors before every pitch.
