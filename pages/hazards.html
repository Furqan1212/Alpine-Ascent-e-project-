Respect the mountain.

Objective dangers

What can go wrong.

Most mountaineering accidents come from a small number of well-known hazards. Learn to recognise them early.

Snow

Avalanches

Check the bulletin, avoid steep loaded slopes, and carry a beacon, probe and shovel.

Ice

Crevasses

Hidden beneath snow bridges, they are the reason glacier travel is always roped.

Altitude

Mountain sickness

Headache and nausea above 2,500 m are warnings. Descending is the only reliable cure.

Rules of thumb

Decide before you climb.

Set turn-around times and honour them, however close the summit looks.

Weather, fatigue and altitude combine quickly. Most fatal mistakes happen on the descent, so keep energy and daylight in reserve.

Questions

Worth knowing.

What is the safest response to altitude sickness?

Do not go higher. Rest and drink water. If symptoms worsen, descend. Descending is the only reliable treatment.

What is a crevasse and why is it dangerous?

A crevasse is a deep crack in a glacier. Snow can hide it, so teams travel roped together to stop a fall.

How can I check avalanche risk?

Read the local avalanche bulletin, avoid steep loaded slopes, and carry a beacon, probe and shovel, and know how to use them.

What should I do if bad weather arrives?

Turn back early. Storms bring whiteouts and cold, and descent is much harder once visibility is lost.
