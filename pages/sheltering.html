A warm place to wait out the storm.

Options

Where climbers sleep.

From a one-night bivouac to a six-week base camp, shelter is chosen to match the altitude, the weather and the plan.

Lightweight

Bivouac sack

A waterproof shell around the sleeping bag for emergency nights or fast alpine routes.

Standard

Four-season tent

Strong poles and a low profile shed wind and snow load on exposed camps.

Natural

Snow cave

Dug into a deep drift, it stays near freezing even as the air outside drops far lower.

Know-how

Digging in safely.

A snow cave can save your life, but only if it is built with care.

Keep the entrance lower than the sleeping platform so cold air drains out, and always poke a ventilation hole through the roof. Check it regularly during snowfall, and keep your shovel inside with you.

  • EntranceBelow the sleeping shelf
  • VentilationAlways keep a roof air hole
  • RoofDomed, about 30 cm thick
  • ToolsKeep shovel and axe inside

Choosing a site

Dig smart, sleep warm.

A good shelter starts with a good location and a plan for the storm you hope never arrives.

  • WhereDig on a stable slope that avalanches cannot reach, with deep, firm snow.
  • WhenBuild early. A cave takes one to two hours and is exhausting after dark.
  • CheckRe-clear the vent and entrance during snowfall, and keep your shovel inside.

Questions

Worth knowing.

How long does a snow cave take to dig?

Plan on one to two hours for a small cave. Digging with a partner is faster and safer.

Can I use a snow cave anywhere?

Only where the snow is deep and firm and the slope is safe from avalanches. Otherwise use a tent or hut.

What if I cannot build a shelter?

Use a bivouac sack, stay dry, sit on your pack, insulate from the ground and keep eating and drinking to stay warm.
